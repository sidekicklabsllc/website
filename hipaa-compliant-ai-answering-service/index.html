What a HIPAA-compliant AI answering service actually means

Updated September 11, 2026

"HIPAA compliant" on a vendor's home page tells you almost nothing. There is no HIPAA certification; the law describes obligations, and a vendor either meets them in specific, checkable ways or it does not. This page explains what those obligations mean for an AI answering service, what to ask any vendor, and how Samantha answers each question.

The short version. An AI answering service that touches patient calls is a business associate. It must sign a BAA, keep protected health information on infrastructure that is itself covered by a BAA, limit what it discloses to the minimum necessary, log access, and retain records only as long as required. Ask to see each of those, not a badge.

Why an answering service is a business associate

A caller who says "I need to move my appointment with Dr. Patel, I'm having more pain" has just disclosed protected health information: their identity, that they are a patient, and a symptom. Anything that hears, stores, transcribes, or relays that call is handling PHI on the practice's behalf. Under the Privacy Rule that makes the service a business associate, and the practice may not share PHI with it until a Business Associate Agreement is in place.

Seven questions to ask any AI answering service

  1. Will you sign a BAA before we go live? If the answer is "we are working on it", stop.
  2. Which cloud services process the audio, the transcript, and the language model, and are those services under a BAA too? Many AI vendors run on consumer-grade APIs that explicitly exclude PHI.
  3. What is in the notification your staff receives? A text message that says "Jane Doe called about her biopsy results" has just sent PHI over SMS.
  4. What does the system log? Debug logs that capture caller speech or full phone numbers are a breach waiting to happen.
  5. How is the caller's identity verified before appointment details are discussed? Caller ID alone is not verification.
  6. How long are recordings and transcripts retained, and who can delete them?
  7. What happens if the system is misconfigured? The honest answer for most products is "it keeps running". Ask what would stop it.

How Samantha answers each one

✓

BAA, signed by Sidekick Labs LLC, before any live patient traffic. Ask for it on the intro call.

✓

BAA-covered infrastructure end to end. Telephony, speech recognition, the language model, and speech synthesis all run on HIPAA-eligible services that are themselves under a BAA. No consumer API keys.

✓

Zero-PHI notifications. Staff alerts say only that a new message or booking exists. The content lives behind login in Samantha Hub. Nothing patient-identifying rides in an SMS or push notification.

✓

Redacted logs. System logs never reproduce caller speech or full phone numbers. Recordings and transcripts are stored separately, encrypted, behind authentication.

✓

Identity before information. Returning callers are recognized by verified caller ID, but no appointment detail is shared or changed until the caller confirms full name and date of birth.

✓

Bounded retention with audit trails. Records are kept only as long as the practice requires, and access is logged to meet HIPAA documentation rules.

✓

Compliance verified in code at startup. If any part of the configuration is not PHI-safe, Samantha refuses to start. A misconfiguration cannot quietly run overnight.

What "HIPAA compliant" does not mean

It does not mean the AI may give medical advice. Samantha never triages, interprets symptoms or results, or makes clinical or billing decisions; anything clinical becomes a message for your staff. It does not mean your practice's own obligations disappear: you still need your own policies, workforce training, and a risk analysis that includes the answering service. And it does not mean a badge. If a vendor cannot walk you through the seven questions above with specifics, the badge is decoration.
