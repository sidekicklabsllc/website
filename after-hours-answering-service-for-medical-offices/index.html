After-hours answering service for medical offices

Updated September 11, 2026

Roughly four in ten calls to a medical practice arrive when the office is closed, and in a 2025 review of 7,000 calls across 22 practices, 42 percent of after-hours calls went unanswered (notifyMD). Each of those is a booking that went to another clinic, a refill that turned into a Monday morning crisis, or a patient who decided you were hard to reach. This page walks through what actually happens between close and open, and what good coverage looks like.

What after-hours callers actually want

The calls that reach a medical office at night fall into a few buckets, and they are not the ones people expect:

  • Scheduling. "Can I get in this week", "I need to move Thursday", "cancel my 9am". The single largest group, and the one a message-only service handles worst.
  • Messages for staff. Refill requests to pass along, a question for the nurse, a callback request. They need to be captured accurately and land in front of the right person in the morning.
  • New patients. Someone who searched at 9pm, found you, and is ready to register. If they reach voicemail, most will not call back.
  • Urgent and emergency calls. A minority, but the highest-stakes minute of the night. The only correct response is to direct the caller to 911 and, where the practice has on-call staff, bring them onto the line. Nothing about this call should involve scheduling or judgment about how serious it is.

The three ways practices cover the night

Voicemail

Free, and the reason 42 percent of calls go unanswered. Patients hang up on voicemail at high rates, and the ones who do leave a message expect a callback the front desk now has to schedule.

A live answering service

A shared operator pool takes messages and pages on-call. Reliable for escalation, weak on scheduling because the operator cannot see your calendar, and priced per minute so the bill moves with volume.

An AI receptionist

Software answers every call immediately, verifies the patient, books into real availability, takes messages, screens for emergencies, and hands the front desk a sorted briefing in the morning. Flat monthly pricing. The trade-off is that it must be built to stay strictly out of clinical territory. A fuller comparison is here.

What a night with Samantha looks like

  1. 6:00 pm. The office forwards its line. Samantha picks up with the practice name and an after-hours greeting.
  2. 7:15 pm. A returning patient wants to move Thursday's visit. Samantha confirms name and date of birth, reads back the current appointment, offers two open slots from the calendar, books one, and reads it back before committing. It is on the schedule before the call ends.
  3. 8:40 pm. A new patient calls after finding the practice online. Samantha registers them by phone, name, date of birth, address, insurance, and books a first visit.
  4. 10:05 pm. A caller describes symptoms that sound like an emergency. Before anything else, Samantha tells them to hang up and dial 911. If the practice has an on-call line configured, she brings it in. She does not triage, and she does not schedule.
  5. 11:30 pm. A patient wants the nurse to call about a refill. Samantha takes the message accurately. Staff receive a notification that a message exists; the content stays behind login.
  6. 7:30 am. The front desk opens the briefing: every call from the night, sorted by urgency, each with a recording, a transcript, and a flag on anything that did not fully complete.

Where the appointments land

Every practice gets Samantha Hub, a web portal with scheduling, messages, and the overnight briefing, so Samantha works from the first night by booking into the slots you hold open for her. Practices on Google Calendar get appointments on the calendar they already use. Direct EMR integrations are available on request; we will tell you plainly what is live and what is not.

Daytime overflow and outbound calls

The same line that covers the night can take the calls your front desk cannot reach during the day, and Samantha can place outbound calls too: reminders, follow-ups, and referral capture, where she calls each referred patient, introduces the practice, registers them, and books the first visit. Identity is verified by date of birth before she says anything, and voicemail gets a callback-only message with no detail in it.
