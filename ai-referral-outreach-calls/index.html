AI referral outreach calls that turn referrals into booked visits

Updated September 11, 2026

A referral is a patient another physician has already decided you should see. It is the warmest lead a practice ever gets, and about a third of them turn into a completed visit. In a study of more than 100,000 referral scheduling attempts in a primary care network, only 35 percent ended in a documented completed appointment (Journal of General Internal Medicine, 2018). Industry estimates put the overall figure at roughly half. The reason is rarely the patient. It is phone tag: the fax arrives, a coordinator calls, gets voicemail, tries twice more over a week, and marks the chart "unable to reach."

What Samantha does with a referral

Your front desk enters the referred patients in Samantha Hub and starts the calls. Samantha calls each one the same day, introduces the practice and the referring provider, confirms she is speaking with the right person before saying anything else, gets them registered, and books their first visit with the provider the referral names. Every call lands in your morning briefing with the recording, the transcript, and a flag on anything that needs a person.

What she will not do. Samantha never discusses why the patient was referred, never answers a clinical question, and never gives medication or triage guidance. If the patient asks, she offers to book the visit or take a message for the clinician. Emergency handling is the same as on every call: hang up and dial 911.

Why a call, and why fast

Referral outreach today is mostly text and email, which patients ignore at rates vendors themselves put at 50 to 70 percent. A phone call from the practice, made the day the referral arrives rather than three to seven days later, is the format that actually converts. The constraint has never been whether calling works. It is that a coordinator's day holds a finite number of calls, and each referral costs 20 to 30 minutes of staff time by most vendor estimates. Samantha removes that constraint: the list gets worked the same afternoon, and staff only touch the calls that need a human.
